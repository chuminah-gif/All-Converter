How to Measure Your Ring Size at Home

All you need is a piece of string or a paper strip to find your size without visiting a store.

Measuring ring size with string or paper

  1. Wrap a piece of string or a paper strip around the finger you'll wear the ring on, and mark where it overlaps. Leave a little slack so it can still pass over your knuckle.
  2. Measure the length up to the mark with a ruler. That length is your finger's circumference in millimeters.
  3. Subtract 43 from the circumference (mm) to get the Korean ring size. For example, a 57mm circumference gives 57 − 43 = size 14.
  4. Enter that size into the ring size converter to instantly see the equivalent US, UK, EU, and Japanese sizes.
If your knuckle is thicker than the base of your finger, measure with enough slack for the ring to pass over it. Sizing to the knuckle keeps the ring comfortable once it's past.

Finger size changes depending on when you measure

Fingers can swell or shrink slightly depending on the time of day, temperature, and even how much salty food you've eaten. Measuring in the afternoon or evening at room temperature — rather than early morning or in the cold — gives a size closer to your everyday fit.

If you plan to wear the ring daily, it's safest to measure when your fingers are at their largest (typically afternoon to evening) so the ring never feels too tight later in the day.

Why ring sizing systems differ by country

CountrySizing basis
Korea/JapanNumeric size based on inner circumference (mm)
USNumeric size (with half sizes) based on inner diameter (inches)
UKAlphabetical size (A–Z, extending to Z+1 etc.) based on inner circumference
EUNumeric size using inner circumference (mm) directly

Because the base unit (diameter vs. circumference) and the notation (numbers vs. letters) differ by country, the same finger can end up with very different-looking size numbers. Use the ring size converter for exact conversions.

Wide bands need a size up

The wider a ring's band, the more surface area it contacts on your finger, making the same nominal size feel tighter. For bands 5mm or wider, it's common to size up by half a size to a full size compared to your measured size.

Frequently asked questions

Q. My fingers are different sizes. Which one should I measure?

Even on the same hand, finger size varies (thumb, index, middle, etc.), and your left and right hand can differ too. Always measure the specific finger you plan to wear the ring on.

Q. How do I find someone else's ring size as a gift, without asking?

Borrow a ring they already wear on a similar finger, trace the inner circle onto paper, and measure the diameter. You can also thread a string or paper strip through the inside of the ring to measure its inner circumference.
