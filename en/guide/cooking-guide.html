Cooking Measurements Explained

How to measure cups, tablespoons and teaspoons accurately, and why measuring units differ by country.

Liquids and dry ingredients are measured differently

For liquids like water, milk, or oil, place a liquid measuring cup on a flat surface and read the marking at eye level. For dry ingredients like flour or sugar, the standard method is to spoon the ingredient into the cup until it mounds up, then level off the top with the back of a knife or a chopstick.

Packing or scooping dry ingredients directly with the cup can make the same "1 cup" noticeably heavier than intended, throwing off the recipe. For baking, where measurement accuracy really matters, sifting the ingredient first and spooning it in gently before leveling gives the most consistent results.

No measuring spoons? Here's a substitute

A standard soup spoon is often close in volume to a tablespoon (15mL), but spoon sizes vary enough that it should only be used as a rough estimate. For recipes where accuracy matters, using actual measuring spoons is safer.

Why do Korean and US measuring cups differ?

Korean measuring cups, tablespoons, and teaspoons are standardized to clean round numbers — 200mL, 15mL, and 5mL. The US, on the other hand, still uses the fluid ounce from the imperial system, where "1 cup = 8 fl oz." Since 1 fl oz is about 29.57mL, this works out to the less tidy figure of about 236.59mL per US cup.

If a recipe — especially a US recipe — just says "1 cup" without specifying, it almost always means the US cup (236.59mL), not the Korean cup (200mL). Selecting "US measuring cup" in the cooking measurement converter and converting to mL is the safest way to translate it accurately.

Making sense of "stick" butter measurements

US recipes often list butter as "1 stick" or "1/2 cup." Butter sold in the US typically comes packaged as sticks, with tablespoon markings printed right on the wrapper — so you can cut off exactly the amount you need without a measuring spoon at all.

Measuring ingredients by weight (grams) removes any error from how tightly you pack the cup, so it's always accurate. For baking, where precision matters most, a kitchen scale is recommended over a measuring cup.

Frequently asked questions

Q. Can I use a regular mug instead of a measuring cup?

Mugs vary widely in capacity — anywhere from 150mL to over 350mL — so results can differ significantly from what the recipe expects. Use a standard measuring cup or scale for baking, where accuracy matters, and reserve mugs for things like soups where small errors don't matter much.

Q. Why does 1 cup of water weigh differently than 1 cup of flour?

A cup measures volume (mL), while weight (g) depends on the ingredient's density. Water is always exactly 1mL = 1g, but flour holds air between its particles, so the same volume weighs less than water.
