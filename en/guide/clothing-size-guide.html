International Clothing Sizes Explained

How to avoid sizing mistakes when shopping across borders.

Where Korean size numbers come from

Korean women's wear numbers like 44, 55, 66, and 77 come from bust measurement (cm) by convention. Men's shirt numbers like 90, 95, and 100 are based on neck circumference (cm). The US and UK, by contrast, use letters (XS–XXL) or a separate numbering system, and the EU uses its own numbers — so the raw numbers aren't directly comparable across systems.

Why actual measurements matter more than the size label

Even the same "M" label can correspond to different actual bust or shoulder measurements depending on the brand or country of manufacture. The size correspondence table this converter provides is a general guideline; when buying from an overseas retailer, it's most accurate to compare the "actual measurements (cm)" chart provided on the product page with your own body measurements.

How to find your actual measurements

  1. Lay a shirt that fits you well flat and measure the chest, shoulder width, sleeve length, and total length with a tape measure.
  2. Compare these numbers with the "actual measurements" chart on the product page you're buying from.
  3. Trust the actual measurements over the labeled size (S, M, L, etc.).
Fit also varies by the brand's country of origin — European brands tend to run slim, while American brands tend to run roomier. Checking actual measurements matters even more when buying from a brand for the first time.

Frequently asked questions

Q. If I'm between two sizes, should I size up or down?

It depends on the garment. For knitwear or outerwear, a slightly looser fit is often fine; for shirts or jackets where fit matters more, sizing closer to your actual measurements usually works better.

Q. Should I check the return policy before ordering?

Cross-border returns are often expensive or difficult, so if the sizing is borderline, it's worth checking the seller's return policy before purchasing.
