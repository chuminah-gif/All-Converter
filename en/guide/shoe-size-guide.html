International Shoe Sizes Explained

How to measure your feet, and why sizing systems vary by country.

How to measure your foot length accurately

  1. Stand on a sheet of paper with your heel against a wall.
  2. Mark the tip of your longest toe and the back of your heel on the paper.
  3. Measure the distance between the two marks — that's your foot length (cm).
  4. Feet tend to swell later in the day, so measuring in the afternoon or evening gives a more realistic size for actual shoe shopping.

Why shoe sizing systems differ by country

Korea (KR) and Japan (JP) use the Mondopoint system, where the shoe size is simply the foot length in millimeters. For example, a 250mm foot is a Korean size 250, or Japanese size 25.0.

The US and UK use a scale based on the old "barleycorn" unit (about 8.5mm per step), with separate ranges for men's and women's sizes. For the same foot length, the US women's size number is typically about 1.5–2 higher than the men's number.

The EU uses a single unisex scale based on the "Paris point" (about 6.7mm per step).

Even the same size number can fit differently across brands due to different lasts (shoe molds). If you have a wide foot, check the brand's width (Wide) options as well.

Frequently asked questions

Q. My feet are slightly different sizes. Which one should I use?

Most people have a small size difference between their feet. It's standard to size based on the larger foot.

Q. Should I size up if I have wide feet?

Rather than sizing up in length, it's usually more accurate to choose a Wide-width option if the brand offers one. Sizing up in length alone can leave excess room at the toe.
