Currency Exchange Explained

Why reference rates and actual exchange rates differ.

What is a reference exchange rate?

A reference exchange rate is a "representative rate" calculated from that day's market trading — the rate you see quoted in the news. This converter uses the daily reference rate published by the European Central Bank (ECB).

Why do bank rates differ from the reference rate?

Banks and exchange booths apply their own fee (spread) on top of the reference rate, quoting separate "buy" and "sell" rates. For example, if the reference rate is 1,320 KRW per USD, a bank might sell dollars at 1,340 and buy them back at 1,300 — that gap is the bank's fee income.

What is a preferential exchange rate?

A "90% preferential rate" shown in a banking app means 90% of that spread (fee gap) is waived. The higher the preferential rate, the closer you get to the true reference rate.

This converter's rates are reference rates for informational purposes. The actual amount you'll receive when exchanging or transferring money depends on your bank's preferential rate and fees — check with your financial institution's app or branch for the exact figure.

Frequently asked questions

Q. How often do exchange rates change?

The reference rate used by this converter updates once per business day. Real-time trading rates at banks and brokerages fluctuate continuously.

Q. When is the best time to exchange currency?

Since exchange rates are notoriously hard to predict, it's often more practical to focus on using a high-preferential-rate channel (mobile app, partner promotion, etc.) when you actually need the currency, rather than trying to time the market.
