How to Measure Your Bra Size with a Tape Measure

Just two measurements — band and bust — are enough to shop for the right size online.

Measuring your band size

  1. Wrap a tape measure horizontally around your ribcage, directly under your bust (the band line). Breathe out normally, and keep the tape snug against your body without pulling it tight.
  2. Round the measurement to the nearest 5cm to get your Korean (= EU/China) band size. For example, 73–77cm rounds to a 75cm band.

Measuring your cup size

  1. Wrap the tape measure horizontally around the fullest part of your bust.
  2. Subtract your band measurement from your bust measurement, then check the difference (cm) against the table below for an approximate cup size.

This table reflects a commonly used rule of thumb — actual fit can vary slightly depending on a brand's pattern and wire shape.

Why band and cup notation differs by country

Korea, the EU, and China express band size in centimeters (in 5cm steps), while the US and UK use inches (in 2-inch steps). The underlying body measurement is the same — only the notation differs.

Cup letters (roughly AA through F) line up the same way across most countries, but notation diverges further up the scale. The UK, for instance, moves to double or special letters as cups get larger — DD, E, F, FF — while the US often repeats a letter instead, as in DDD. So the same actual size can look different depending on which country's labels you're reading.

Wire angle and fabric stretch vary by brand, so the same labeled size can fit differently. When trying a brand for the first time, it's worth checking reviews or the exchange policy before committing.

Frequently asked questions

Q. I don't have a tape measure. What can I use instead?

A piece of string or a belt wrapped around your body, then measured against a ruler, works as a substitute. It's less precise than a proper flexible tape measure, so use a real tape measure if you can.

Q. Why does the same size feel different across brands?

Even with identical labeled sizes, differences in wire angle, cup cut, and fabric stretch between brands can change the actual fit. Treat the labeled size as a starting point and adjust based on how it actually feels once you try it on.
