KB, MB, GB, TB Explained

How data storage units relate to each other, and why your storage device shows less space than what's printed on the box.

How KB, MB, GB, and TB Relate

Data storage units scale by 1,024 (2¹⁰), not 1,000, because computers work in binary internally. This site's data storage converter uses the same 1,024-based scale that computers actually calculate with.

UnitEqual to
1 KB (kilobyte)1,024 bytes
1 MB (megabyte)1,024 KB
1 GB (gigabyte)1,024 MB
1 TB (terabyte)1,024 GB

"I bought 128GB — why does it show 118GB?"

Phones, SSDs, and other storage devices often report noticeably less usable space than the number printed on the box. That's not a defect — it comes down to which base is being used.

  • Storage manufacturers typically advertise capacity using 1GB = 1,000MB (a decimal, base-1,000 count) for marketing.
  • Operating systems (Windows, Android, etc.) calculate and display capacity using 1GB = 1,024MB (binary, base-1,024).

The exact same 128,000,000,000 bytes reads as "128GB" under the decimal convention but only about 119.2GB once divided using the binary convention. The gap between the two grows as capacity increases, which is why it feels especially large on terabyte-class drives.

Bits and Bytes Are Not the Same

Internet plan "speed" and file "size" are quoted in different units.

  • Internet speed: quoted in Mbps (megabits per second)
  • File size: quoted in MB (megabytes)

Since 1 byte = 8 bits, a "100Mbps" connection tops out at 100 ÷ 8 = 12.5MB per second in actual download speed. Mixing up bits and bytes is one of the most common reasons a connection feels slower than its advertised speed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. What are KiB and MiB — I've seen those too.

KiB (kibibyte) and MiB (mebibyte) are the official international units created specifically to mean the binary, 1,024-based count without ambiguity. In practice, though, the computing industry still mostly uses "KB" and "MB" to mean the 1,024-based values by convention.

Q. Roughly how big is one photo, or one movie file?

A smartphone photo is typically around 2–5MB depending on resolution, and a compressed HD movie is often somewhere around 1.5–4GB. Actual file size varies a lot with resolution, compression, and the device that captured it.

Q. Do cloud storage plans use the same GB?

Most cloud services also quote GB and TB, but whether they use the 1,000-based or 1,024-based convention can differ by provider, so check your specific service's documentation for the exact usable capacity.
